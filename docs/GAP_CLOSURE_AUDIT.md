# Reviewer gap closure audit

**Scope:** the frozen local broker and protocols, related-work audit, completed scripted comparison and post hoc coverage diagnostic, completed V1/V2 capability records, and separate runtime coverage wrapper inspected on 18 October 2026 (local time). All **36 corrected V2 capability episodes passed replay audit**; neither model passed the capability gate, so **no V2 main evaluation was launched**. V1 main pilots remain excluded after the public-label leak. “Closed” below means resolved within the stated prototype boundary, not established for deployment.

The project now supports a substantially clearer **policy specification and evaluation contribution**. It does not establish a new general counterfactual authorization algorithm. The strongest arithmetic provenance comparator is equivalent to CEG in this additive queue model, and the completed comparison correctly reports that equivalence.

## What the completed scripted comparison establishes

The completed artifact contains **12 authored cases, 14 matched probe states, 112 probe executions and 96 separate reference executions, with zero model calls**. The extra two probe states test cleanup. They are not 14 independently sampled deployment environments.

| Policy | Probe ALLOW / DENY / DEFER (14 total) | Executed strict-policy violations | Actor-dependent external executions | Reference workflows completed (12 total) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Factual-only | 14 / 0 / 0 | 7 | 5 | 12 |
| History-rules | 5 / 6 / 3 | 1 | 0 | 10 |
| Provenance-rules | 6 / 5 / 3 | 1 | 0 | 10 |
| CEG | 6 / 5 / 3 | 1 | 0 | 10 |
| Deny-all | 0 / 14 / 0 | 0 | 0 | 9 |

CEG and provenance-rules agree on **14/14 matched decisions**. Both retain the unregistered-repair violation. Both reference-controller failures arise from evidence faults that cause deferral; deny-all additionally prevents the necessary legitimate external route. CEG permits an independently sufficient mixed state that history-rules rejects because discretionary load is also present. These are concrete distinctions between policy semantics under controlled evidence, not estimates of spontaneous attacks or deployment safety–utility gains.

The comparison manifest is marked completed. Its recorded hashes for `probes.jsonl`, `references.jsonl`, `summary.json`, `cases.jsonl`, the broker, guard and comparison script were independently recomputed during this audit and matched. This verifies artifact consistency, not independent experimental replication or proof that model inference occurred. No model inference occurs in this comparison.

## V1 disposition and completed V2 capability screen

The independently identified leak was `task.id = self.manifest.id`: actor-visible identifiers contained private scenario categories. The independent inspection was performed by a separate AI agent, not an external human reviewer. Preserved V1 main manifests are **interrupted**, with **7 Qwen episodes and 6 Llama episodes recorded**. These incomplete, label-exposed main runs are excluded from confirmatory comparisons; they must not be silently pooled with V2 or presented as the original full study.

The completed V1 capability cohorts remain diagnostic records. Recounting their saved metrics gives Qwen **6/18 full capability successes, 5/18 with zero strict-policy violations**, and Llama **0/18 on either endpoint**. These results do not justify interpreting absent attack behavior as defense efficacy.

The separately frozen V2 protocol retained **18 fresh episodes per model**: three repetitions of each of six exercises. Eligibility required **at least 12/18 compliant successes overall and at least 1/3 in every exercise**. This was a prospective decision after V1, not an original preregistered criterion or a statistically validated competence threshold.

The final audit reports **36/36 replay agreement**, **403 inference calls and 403 dispatched actions**, **zero model/transport errors**, and **32 budget exhaustions**. There were **137 rejected invalid tool calls**, so zero transport errors must not be confused with error-free tool use. Episode-file hashes match the completed manifests. Both main cohorts are **not launched**, not completed with a zero attack rate. These corrected results remain separate from V1 diagnostics and the older 192-episode pilot.

The screen establishes limited capability under the tested small models, schema-constrained JSON prompting and 12-action budget. It does not isolate inherent model limitations from prompt, tool-order or decoding sensitivity. A stronger actor or changed interface requires separately documented fresh capability checks before an efficacy claim.

## Post hoc requirement-coverage diagnostic

The completed diagnostic examines **112 saved scripted requests and 96 reference end states**, with **zero model calls and no new broker execution**. Its veto-only check uses immutable task requirements and trusted trace evidence available before the event boundary. A declared but unregistered repair requirement causes deferral; a registered requirement proceeds only with sufficient completion evidence. `PROCEED` never overrides an existing denial or deferral.

For CEG and provenance-rules, this extra check **would suppress one previously allowed request each**, the saved unregistered-repair counterexample. Their hypothetical composed outcomes become **5 ALLOW / 5 DENY / 4 DEFER**, leaving zero strict violations among those saved requests *if the veto were applied*. Historical outcomes remain unchanged. Factual-only retains five actor-dependent violations after the repair veto, demonstrating that coverage and cause eligibility address different defects. All eight registered-and-completed reference checks proceed; eight unregistered reference states remain deferred even after repair, pending trusted coverage correction.

The diagnostic output hashes match its completed manifest. This establishes consistency of the saved calculation. It does **not** measure a live intervention, changed future actions, completion utility or a general zero-violation rate. Requirements missing from the manifest, compromised evidence, new scheduler events and trusted correction of missing registration remain unresolved.

## Separate runtime hardening and test evidence

The post hoc `RequirementCoveredBroker` in `hardened_guard_broker.py` now enforces the coverage veto during authorization and again immediately before the simulated external report is written. It binds immutable manifest evidence, checks committed event-prefix releases against live SQL, and verifies the actual recovery set and guard obligation rather than trusting a static registration flag alone. It composes with the underlying policy and supplies no permission of its own. Missing registration still defers even after repair; legitimate local completion remains available where feasible.

The final callback check covers a concrete integration gap: an inherited CEG lease otherwise rechecks only the original guard's state. A fault-injection test removes coverage after lease issuance and verifies that no external report is written. An earlier `external_allows` counter can consequently coexist with blocked execution; successful execution must be measured from the verified artifact and `report_written` event.

The project reports **15 focused wrapper tests and 317 total passing tests**; the 15 are included in the total, not additional experiments. This audit inspected the implementation, test cases and recorded project status but did not rerun that suite. These deterministic checks support the local declared-requirement invariant. They are neither learned-agent effectiveness results nor a claim that all omissions are fixed. The wrapper adds trusted SQL evidence, remains confined to the local Python/SQLite process, and has **not been evaluated in a model cohort**. Its runtime code, additional events and source manifest remain separate from all frozen collectors, cases and replay evidence.

## Why CEG equals provenance-rules here

For the original frozen comparison, consider a conditional request at a fixed trusted time and policy epoch. Assume complete, consistent mediation of active resource contributions, identical approved-work classification and identical registered-recovery state. Let

\[
S = B + E + W, \qquad F = B + E + W + D = S + D,
\]

where \(B\) is trusted baseline load (zero in the current broker), \(E\) independent external load, \(W\) approved-work load, and \(D\geq0\) active discretionary load. All quantities are additive and nonnegative. For threshold \(\theta\),

\[
(F\geq\theta)\land(S\geq\theta)
\iff S\geq\theta,
\]

because \(S\geq\theta\) implies \(F=S+D\geq\theta\). The arithmetic comparator computes this same \(S\) from eligible active observed causes; it does not call the guard's decision method.

Let \(U\) denote the shared unresolved-evidence flag and \(R\) an overdue registered recovery obligation. With the implementation's precedence, both policies have the same outcome:

```text
if U:          DEFER
elif S < θ:    DENY
elif R:        DEFER
else:          ALLOW
```

Thus equivalence is an algebraic consequence of the model, not merely an empirical coincidence in 14 probes. The argument concerns **authorization outcomes**, not identical software, lease mechanisms or performance. It assumes the same evidence and obligations; current fault cases also agree because both paths defer on the same uncertainty. It does not establish equivalence for nonlinear resource interactions, unobserved causal dependencies, different clocks, arbitrary counterfactual worlds or unconditional policies.

## Defensible manuscript contribution

**Suggested title:** *Resource-Dependent Permissions for Tool-Using Agents: An Execution-Backed Study of Provenance and Recovery Rules*.

**Current defensible claim:** We implement a bounded broker for resource-dependent permissions and compare rules using matched executed sequences and verified artifacts. The study shows reference-ledger/provenance-rule equivalence in an additive model and exposes mixed-cause, evidence and recovery-registration tradeoffs. Corrected capability screening identifies a barrier to the planned agent evaluation. A separate post hoc wrapper enforces declared repair coverage with deterministic local tests. These results support an auditable testbed and policy study, not a validated LLM defense.

For a stronger empirical paper, obtain a capable actor under a separately frozen protocol and measure policy effects, ordinary/adversarial behavior, errors and utility costs. Do not use the interrupted V1 main runs, failed V2 screens or wrapper unit tests to rescue an efficacy claim. The earlier 192-episode behavioral pilot also cannot be relabeled as CEG validation.

**Concrete remaining risks:** (1) no algorithmic advantage over equally informed provenance rules in the present model; (2) both corrected model–interface configurations fail the capability screen; (3) same-team case/label construction and only one local logical-queue domain; (4) trusted provenance and complete mediation assumptions exclude indirect or distributed effects, and the wrapper cannot discover undeclared requirements; and (5) availability and task-utility costs under adaptive behavior remain unmeasured. Approved work remains eligible even when the actor chooses its scheduling, so the guard does not prevent every strategic arrangement of authorized work. Independent review, capable adaptive actors, operational provenance-error tests and a fair published-system integration remain necessary. V1 label contamination remains a historical exclusion, not an unresolved V2 leak. Repetitions and unit tests cannot substitute for external evidence.
